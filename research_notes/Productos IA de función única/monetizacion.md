# Monetization and growth patterns of single-purpose AI tools ("AI wrappers"), 2023–2026

Scope note: research conducted 2026-11-09. All figures are dated inline. "Estimate", "anecdote" and "self-reported" flags are used where the number does not come from an audited or primary disclosure. Sources behind paywalls (Kyle Poyar's full 35-page report, ChartMogul full report) were read via their public summary pages or secondary coverage, and are flagged as such.

---

## Key question 1 — For narrow AI tools, is credits, subscription or hybrid the dominant model, and what evidence supports it?

### Takeaway
Across every dataset found (Poyar/Growth Unhinged 2025 survey of 240+ B2B software/AI companies, Metronome's 50-company Pricing Index 2026, a16z's Top-100 consumer AI ranking 2026), the dominant pattern is **hybrid: a recurring subscription that bundles a monthly credit allowance, with optional credit top-ups**. Pure pay-per-use credits and pure flat subscriptions are both minorities; the direction of travel 2024→2026 is away from flat/seat pricing toward hybrid, driven explicitly by compute-cost protection.

### Cited Findings
- Hybrid pricing rose from 27% to 41% of surveyed companies in twelve months (survey April–May 2025, 240+ software and AI companies); flat-fee subscriptions fell from 29% to 22% and seat-based from 21% to 15%. Only 5% say their primary model is outcome-based today, 25% expect it by 2028. Respondents cited "internal costs and margins" as the most important factor when pricing AI capabilities. Sample skew: 26% <$1M ARR, 22% $1–5M, 15% $5–20M, 37% >$20M. — [Kyle Poyar, 2025 State of B2B Monetization (Growth Unhinged), 2025](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/2025-state-of-b2b-monetization)
- Metronome's Pricing Index (50+ companies, published 2 April 2026) concludes "single-track pricing models are becoming the minority; hybrid is the norm" and that "nearly every category" has some credit model. Named patterns: Runway = credit-based subscription for the creative app plus credit-per-operation for API; Midjourney = tiers differentiated by GPU time; ElevenLabs = character usage with credit pools scaling across tiers; Lovable = fixed monthly credit pool that refills; Windsurf moved *from* credits to fixed quotas because users wanted cost predictability; Cursor changed pricing structure four times in under two years with credit pools scaling from $20 to $200. — [Metronome, "2026 trends from cataloging 50 AI pricing models", 2026-04-02](https://metronome.com/blog/2026-trends-from-cataloging-50-ai-pricing-models)
- a16z's seventh Top-100 Consumer Gen-AI Apps report (2026): among the 44 AI-native products in the web ranking, 84% offer subscriptions, 64% use usage charges or extra credits, 14% advertising, 2% transaction/platform fees; high model operating costs pushed developers to introduce subscriptions and usage fees earlier than in previous consumer-internet categories. Among AI payers, median spend was $25/month; the top 1% of payers averaged $903/month. Only ~4.5% of eligible US consumers in YipitData's panel paid for ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude. (Secondary coverage of the a16z report.) — [ForkLog on a16z, 2026](https://forklog.com/en/a16z-details-u-s-consumer-spending-on-ai-services/); [The Neuron on a16z, 2026](https://theneuron.ai/news/ai-top-1-percent-spend-903-a-month-a16z-data/)
- a16z's Olivia Moore (TechCrunch interview, 9 Oct 2026): "nearly all AI revenue so far comes from subscriptions and token usage"; 2.2% of US households pay for AI (a16z State of Markets); ChatGPT "Go" plan at $8/month. — [TechCrunch, 2026-10-09](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/09/a16zs-olivia-moore-on-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- Credit-based models grew 126% in 2025 vs prior year among the top 500 AI/SaaS companies (PricingSaaS figure cited second-hand by HubSpot; not verified against original). 78% of IT leaders reported surprise AI-related charges in the past year (HubSpot buyer's guide). — [HubSpot, AI credits buyer's guide, 2025/2026](https://blog.hubspot.com/website/ai-credits-buyers-guide)
- Bain (October 2025, 30+ SaaS vendors reviewed): ~65% had added a usage meter on top of a base subscription (second-hand citation). — [Collab365 summary, 2025](https://spaces.collab365.com/posts/ai-saas-makers-shift-to-credits-caps-and-hybrid-pr-j8TMcD)
- Stage 2 Capital / Kyle Poyar (15 Nov 2025): credits are "a bridge toward value-based pricing, not the end state"; "no one wants to manage 20 different credit systems"; the top 10% of users may drive 70–80% of AI costs; recommended: tiers with caps plus overage, treat AI usage as a variable cost, set discounting rules. — [Stage 2 Capital, "Credits Aren't a Strategy", 2025-11-15](https://gtm.stage2.capital/p/credits-arent-a-strategy-rethinking)
- Vellum (founder post, 2 Sept 2026) ran an A/B test 23 Jul–17 Aug 2026: at the exhausted-credit wall, free users were shown "Upgrade" (to plan selection) instead of "Add Credits". Credit-purchase cash fell 44% but overall revenue rose 28% over ~3.5 weeks. Recommended design: subscription prices the durable capability + included monthly credits; credits meter only variable usage (inference, image generation, third-party APIs); auto-reload and pay-as-you-go overflow remain. Principle: "Price in the unit your customer understands. Meter in the unit your infrastructure requires." No conversion or churn rates reported. — [Vellum, 2026-09-02](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/why-subscriptions-beat-credits-for-ai-products)
- Counter-anecdote (Reddit r/SaaS, ~March 2025): founder of svgapp.ai claims switching to full credit-based pricing "increased signup-to-paid conversion 3x" and sells a $5 one-time plan as a trial; another commenter runs 5–15 free chats unsigned, 30–45 signed-in, and a $1.99 credit reset. Self-reported, no cohort data. — [Reddit snapshot, 2025/2026](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qa4zea/snapshots/2026-01-12T05%3A51%3A17.644725Z)
- Single-founder data points on pure subscription for narrow AI tools: Photo AI (Pieter Levels) is subscription-only, main Pro plan $29/month for 1,000 generated images, with annual discounts; $132K/month revenue disclosed May 2025 (the article notes this is monthly revenue, not strict MRR, because many pay annually); earlier $100K/month milestone September 2024. Levels says he would charge more than $10/month and calls free users "horrible" because of abuse. — [PPC Land, 2025-05-25](https://ppc.land/how-one-photo-ai-app-generates-132k-monthly-after-70-failed-startups/)
- Midjourney (pure tiered subscription, no free tier since March 2023): estimated ~$500M revenue/ARR in 2025 with no outside funding (third-party estimates; Holz has only confirmed revenue "significantly surpassed $200M"). — [Latka estimate, 2025](https://getlatka.com/companies/midjourney); [TBPN digest, 2026-03-17](https://tbpn-digest.vercel.app/story/2026-03-17/midjourney-hits-500m-arr-with-zero-outside-funding)
- Paid MCP tool example of hybrid-lite: Ref (docs search MCP) — free 200 credits that never expire, $9/month for 1,000 credits, extra 1,000-credit blocks at $9, 1 credit per tool call; "thousands of weekly users" and "hundreds of subscribers" ~3 months after launch (self-reported). — [PulseMCP, 2025-09-10](https://www.pulsemcp.com/posts/pricing-the-unknown-a-paid-mcp-server)

### Inferences
- For a narrow generative tool with real per-use GPU cost, the market consensus 2025–2026 is: subscription tiers that include a credit allowance (cost control + predictable revenue) plus top-ups (captures power users without losing them). Pure prepaid credits are used as an *entry* mechanic (small one-time packs, $5–$10) to convert hesitant users, then migrate them to plans.
- Evidence that credits alone *outperform* subscription is anecdotal (one Reddit founder); evidence that subscription-first outperforms credit-first is also thin (one Vellum A/B test, 3.5 weeks). Neither is a benchmark; both agree on the hybrid end state.
- "Outcome-based" pricing is talked about far more than practiced (5% in 2025).

### Gaps
- No dataset isolates *single-purpose consumer/prosumer AI tools* (as opposed to B2B SaaS or the top-100 consumer apps). The Poyar survey is B2B-weighted; a16z covers only top-100 apps.
- Stripe, Paddle and ProfitWell did not surface any 2024–2026 report specific to AI-tool pricing-model mix in these searches; Paddle's 2026 pricing guide (Phil Carter) exists but was not fetched.
- The HubSpot/PricingSaaS "126% growth in credit models" figure could not be traced to the original dataset.

## Key question 2 — What free→paid conversion and churn benchmarks exist for AI tools (2024–2026)? Credit-based vs subscription differences?

### Takeaway
The best primary-ish benchmarks are the ChartMogul/Growth Unhinged Conversion Report (Jan–Feb 2026, 200 B2B products) — AI-native products convert free→paid at 6–8% (median) and 15–20% (top quartile), slightly *better* than classic SaaS — and RevenueCat's 2026 State of Subscription Apps (115k+ apps) — AI apps convert trials 52% better (8.5% vs 5.6%) and earn 41% more per payer, but retain much worse (annual retention 21.1% vs 30.7%; AI annual subs churn 30% faster) and refund ~20% more. No source quantifies churn for credit-pack buyers vs subscribers; that comparison is a gap.

### Cited Findings
**Free→paid conversion**
- ChartMogul/Growth Unhinged/ProductLed SaaS Conversion Report (survey Jan 2026, published 4 Feb 2026, 200 B2B software products): AI-native and hybrid products convert "slightly better than traditional SaaS": GOOD 6–8%, GREAT 15–20%. AI-native products are more likely to be freemium; 61% of SaaS products use free trial as the primary landing point vs 51% of hybrids and 43% of AI-native. — [ChartMogul Conversion Report, 2026](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/); [Growth Unhinged summary, 2026](https://growthunhinged.com/p/free-to-paid-conversion-report)
- Same report, by model (50th / 75th percentile): standard freemium 3–5% / 8–12%; ungated freemium (no account) 7–9% / 8–12%; free trial no card 4–6% / 10–15%; reverse trial 4–6% / 8–12%; free trial card-required 25–35% / 50–60%. Per 1,000 visitors: freemium 90 signups → ~5 paying; ungated freemium 70 → 5.6; standard trial 45 → 3.6; card-required trial 35 → 10.5. Overall median free→paid 8%; bimodal — ~20% of products convert <2.5%, ~23% convert >25%. — [Userpilot table citing ChartMogul, 2026](https://userpilot.com/blog/free-trial-conversion-rate/)
- RevenueCat 2026 State of Subscription Apps (data from 75k+ developers, >1B transactions, >$11B/yr developer revenue; 27.1% of subscription apps classified as AI-powered): median trial-to-paid 8.5% (AI) vs 5.6% (non-AI), ~52% better; median download-to-paid 2.4% vs 2.0%. — [TechCrunch on RevenueCat, 2026-03-10](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows)
- SaaStr (early 2026) rough math: ChatGPT ~2% free→paid (800M WAU, 12–15M paying consumers); Perplexity 2–3%; RevenueCat mobile average 1.7%; a $100M freemium business at $10/month and 2% conversion needs ~50M active users. — [SaaStr, "Freemium is back: the AI edition", 2026](https://www.saastr.com/freemium-is-back-the-ai-edition-but-youll-still-probably-need-50-million-active-users-for-freemium-to-actually-work-as-a-business-model/)
- Anonymous-first AI image tool zsky.ai (founder post, April–May 2026): ~40% of daily generations come from users who never sign up; anonymous→registered conversion ~9.3%; >52,000 registered users after ~8 months; signup→paid not disclosed. — [dev.to / zsky.ai, 2026](https://dev.to/zsky/the-anonymous-first-product-bet-why-we-let-40-of-our-users-never-sign-up-3l7g)
- Paid crypto-data MCP server (self-reported, April 2026): free→paid "around 8%" at $19/month; costs $14/month data API + Cloudflare Worker; cited 80% gross margin; no MRR disclosed. — [dev.to, 2026-04](https://dev.to/whoffagents/pricing-an-mcp-server-in-2026-why-we-charge-19mo-when-the-market-average-is-0-nig)

**Churn / retention**
- RevenueCat 2026: annual retention 21.1% (AI) vs 30.7% (non-AI); 12-month retention of monthly subs 6.1% vs 9.5%; weekly 2.5% vs 1.7% (the only metric where AI leads, and one secondary source gives 1.2%, so treat weekly as disputed); AI annual subscriptions churn 30% faster at the median; median refund rate 4.2% vs 3.5% (~20% higher), upper bound 15.6% vs 12.5%; realized LTV at median: monthly $18.92 vs $13.59, annual $30.16 vs $21.37 (AI apps earn ~41% more per payer). — [TechCrunch, 2026-03-10](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows); [PPC Land, 2026](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)
- Secondary summary of the same report: "months 3 through 6 are where AI apps bleed subscribers." — [AIchief, 2026](https://aichief.com/news/ai-apps-fail-to-retain-users-long-term-report-finds/)
- Non-AI baselines for context: Recurly 2025 median B2B SaaS annual churn 3.5% (2.6% voluntary + 0.8% involuntary); Focus Digital 2025 table: B2C SaaS 5.8% average monthly churn vs 3.2% B2B (page internally inconsistent). — [Recurly 2025](https://recurly.com/blog/2025-state-of-subscriptions-sneak-peek); [Focus Digital 2025](https://focus-digital.co/average-churn-rate-subscription-services/)
- Mootion (AI animation/video tool; AppSumo case study published 11 June 2026): lifetime-deal users consumed 20–30% more credits than regular subscribers in the first 2–3 months, then settled to subscriber-like levels; estimated active lifetime of AppSumo users ~15–18 months, matching AppSumo platform-wide figures; MRR grew to $100K–$150K and product is profitable. — [AppSumo blog, 2026-06-11](https://appsumo.com/blog/mootion)
- Credits-specific churn mechanics (qualitative): Vellum — manual top-ups create "recurring chances to churn"; auto-reload makes credits behave like a subscription. A dev.to founder's 8-month experiment reports churn "spiked every time a balance hit zero" (post now 404, only the search snippet survives). — [Vellum, 2026-09-02](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/why-subscriptions-beat-credits-for-ai-products); [dev.to snippet, 2026](https://dev.to/gozel_t_8f2c084ded7672955/per-credit-vs-subscription-pricing-my-8-month-mistake-29e3)
- Reddit AI form-builder founder (~March 2025): on a $15/month subscription "many users churned after ~2 months"; most users built 2–3 forms and used AI ~once a month, so he moved to free forms + pay-per-AI-use credits. No post-change numbers. — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qa4zea/snapshots/2026-01-12T05%3A51%3A17.644725Z)

### Inferences
- For a narrow AI tool, a realistic planning range is: free→paid 3–8% (freemium), 6–8% if AI-native per ChartMogul, 25%+ only with card-required trials; expect subscription retention roughly a third worse than a non-AI app of the same type (RevenueCat).
- The AI pattern "monetizes fast, churns fast" favors (a) annual plans with heavy discounts to lock in value before month 3–6 churn, (b) one-time credit packs for episodic users who would otherwise churn a subscription after one job, and (c) auto-reload to turn credit buyers into quasi-subscribers.
- Products with episodic jobs-to-be-done (a headshot, a 3D model of one product catalogue) should expect subscriber churn after the job is done; Mootion's 15–18-month active lifetime for LTD buyers is an upper reference, not a norm.

### Gaps
- No source provides churn or repeat-purchase rates for one-time credit-pack buyers vs subscribers in AI tools. Only qualitative claims (Vellum, dev.to) exist.
- No benchmark splits free→paid conversion by *credit-based vs subscription* pricing for AI tools specifically; ChartMogul splits by trial model, RevenueCat by AI vs non-AI.
- Prepaid-credit breakage (share of purchased credits never used) is undocumented for AI tools; only hypothetical accounting examples (10%, 30%) were found. — [Zenskar glossary](https://www.zenskar.com/finance-glossary/credit-breakage)
- Per-product churn claims (ChatGPT 25%, Claude 55%→36%, Gemini ~42%) circulate from Appfigures/Sensor Tower via aggregators but were not verified and use incompatible definitions.

## Key question 3 — Which products offer "pay only if satisfied" / approval-based billing, and how do they contain cost?

### Takeaway
True "pay only for results you keep" is rare and takes three forms: (1) **pay-on-download credits** (Hyper3D/Rodin: credits only spent when you download), (2) **failed-generation refunds** (Meshy charges nothing for failed tasks and refunds cancelled ones), and (3) **unconditional time-boxed money-back guarantees** on one-time purchases (HeadshotPro: 30 days, "no conditions", self-serve). Cost containment comes from the generation itself being cheap relative to price, from capping what's visible before payment (previews/watermarks/low-res), and in human-in-the-loop services (Kaedim) from pricing per asset at ~$20 with revisions as the remedy rather than refunds.

### Cited Findings
- Hyper3D / Rodin: Free plan is "pay-by-result": credits cost $1.50 each and "are only spent on download"; 10 one-time starter credits; Creator $15/month (7-day trial), Business $60/month or $96/month billed yearly (as listed by aggregator, checked ~Nov 2026). — [Costbench Kaedim vs Rodin, 2026](https://www.costbench.com/compare/kaedim-vs-rodin-hyper3d/)
- Meshy: "No credits are charged for failed generations"; "Credits are refunded for canceled tasks"; Remesh and Rigging cost 0 credits; failed-task policy is the only output-quality guarantee — there is no satisfaction-based refund. — [Meshy docs, pricing and credits, 2026](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/pricing)
- HeadshotPro (one-time purchase AI headshots): "Every purchase is covered by a 30-day money-back guarantee", "No conditions", self-serve via profile/refund page, processed within ~1 business day; page concedes "most photos won't be keepers". A third-party review describes a "Realism Guarantee: redo or full refund" and a FAQ version lists conditions (no downloads, <14 days) — the two HeadshotPro pages conflict, so the current policy should be checked. — [HeadshotPro refund page, 2026](https://www.headshotpro.com/refund); [HeadshotPro refund policy FAQ](https://www.headshotpro.com/docs/individual/billing/refund-policy)
- BetterPic (AI headshots): full refund only before the model is trained; 10% withheld once trained; nothing once a single image is downloaded (third-party review, 2026). — [Fritz.ai BetterPic review, 2026](https://fritz.ai/betterpic-review/)
- Headshot.ltd: "Pay once from $8. No subscription. Money back guarantee." (Product Hunt listing, mechanics not described). — [Product Hunt, Headshot.ltd](https://www.producthunt.com/products/headshot-ltd)
- Kaedim (image→3D with human-in-the-loop, "over 80 professional 3D artists" per a directory; unverified): Trial $50 one-time for 5 credits (geometries), Indie $400/month for 20 credits, Pro $1,200/month for 60 credits (~$20 per geometry, calculated); no published quality guarantee — reviewers describe revision requests as the remedy; turnaround cited as 1–3 business days. A 2023 report said QC workers were paid as little as $1–$4 per model (dated, second-hand). — [Costbench Kaedim, 2026](https://www.costbench.com/compare/kaedim-vs-rodin-hyper3d/); [Toolradar Kaedim](https://toolradar.com/tools/kaedim/pricing); [TechSpot 2023](https://www.techspot.com/news/100076-startup-ai-made-3d-models-2d-art-actually.html)
- AppSumo lifetime deals carry a platform-level 60-day money-back guarantee; Octolens' founder reports gross ~$200K in 60 days but net payouts "closer to $40,000" after AppSumo's ~70% share *and refunds* (refund rate not stated). — [Octolens, 2024-12-12](https://octolens.com/blog/200k-in-60-days-honest-review-of-appsumo-select-for-my-ai-b2b-saas-product-2024)
- RevenueCat 2026: AI apps' median refund rate 4.2% vs 3.5% for non-AI; upper bound 15.6% — the cost of generous refund terms is measurable. — [TechCrunch, 2026-03-10](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows)

### Inferences
- "Pay on download" (Rodin) is the cleanest approval-based model for 3D: generation cost is sunk, but the user only pays for the asset they accept; it works because per-model inference cost is cents to low dollars (see margins section) against $1.50/credit.
- Unconditional 30-day refunds (HeadshotPro) are affordable only when compute per order is small relative to a $29–$49 one-time price and refund rates stay in single digits (RevenueCat median 4.2%).
- For a 3D/AR tool with a human-review step, Kaedim's structure (prepaid credits per geometry, revisions not refunds, ~$20/asset) is the closest precedent; a guarantee should be framed as "revision until accepted" rather than money back.

### Gaps
- No founder disclosed the refund/redo rate under a satisfaction guarantee, nor the effect on conversion of adding one.
- No evidence found of "approval-based billing" in Shopify 3D/AR apps specifically (they bill per plan/model-count, not per accepted model).

## Key question 4 — How do Shopify 3D/AR apps price, and how many installs/reviews do the top ones have?

### Takeaway
Shopify 3D/AR is a small, thinly reviewed category: the most-reviewed apps have ~100 reviews (Angle 3D Configurator 5.0★/101; Zakeke 4.5★/66–111 depending on cached page), most viewer apps have 0–30 reviews, and install counts are not shown publicly on listings. Pricing is per-plan by model count ($10–$59 entry, $99–$399 mid, with "free plan = 1 model" common), sometimes with usage-based generation charges billed outside Shopify. Shopify's revenue share is 0% on the first $1M lifetime gross (revenue from 1 Jan 2025 onward), then 15%, plus a 2.9% processing fee.

### Cited Findings
**Revenue share and store economics**
- Shopify App Store: 0% revenue share on the first $1,000,000 USD of *lifetime* gross app revenue (counting only revenue earned on or after 1 Jan 2025), then 15%; developers with ≥$20M App Store revenue in the prior year or ≥$100M company revenue pay 15% on everything; threshold "doesn't reset each year"; calculated on gross sales, refunds not deducted; one-time $19 registration per Partner account; separate 2.9% processing fee plus applicable taxes. — [Shopify dev docs, revenue share, 2026](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share)
- The $1M exemption was annual from 2021 until Shopify converted it to a lifetime threshold effective 16 June 2025 (press) / revenue from 1 Jan 2025 (docs). — [BetaKit, 2025](https://betakit.com/shopify-app-developers-will-no-longer-be-exempt-from-sharing-their-first-1-million-usd-in-revenue-every-year/)
- App Store size (snapshot 25 May 2026): 17,891 active apps, 11,352 developer partners, 387 new apps in 30 days, 194 removed; 2,139 fully free, 6,147 freemium (~46% with a free plan); average rating 4.70; ~45 reviews per app on average; 1,443 Built-for-Shopify apps (~8%), 98% of featured apps are BFS. Review totals on the page are internally inconsistent (807k vs 999k). — [AppJubilee Shopify App Store Report 2026](https://www.appjubilee.io/shopify-app-store-report-2026)
- Built for Shopify apps "appear higher in Shopify App Store search rankings", get a search filter and a priority review queue; October 2025 changelog made BFS badges clickable and more prominent. — [Shopify changelog, 2025](https://shopify.dev/changelog/built-for-shopify-apps-get-priority-visibility-across-the-shopify-app-store); [Shopify docs, Built for Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/built-for-shopify.md)
- Third-party trackers claim review *velocity* matters more than total count and that BFS apps have a median of 891.5 reviews vs 425 for non-BFS (correlation; Shopify has never published its ranking algorithm). — [AppJubilee blog](https://blog.appjubilee.io/shopify-app-search-rankings-impact-reviews); [Taylor Sicard, 2026](https://taylorsicard.com/blog/is-built-for-shopify-worth-it)

## Key question 5 — Is there evidence of MCP/agent-driven distribution generating paying users for tools (2025–2026)?

### Takeaway
As of late 2026 there is **no independently verified case** of MCP/agent directories driving a meaningful number of paying users to a tool. The documented pattern is that existing API businesses (Bright Data, Exa, Tavily, Context7, Apify actors) expose their already-metered APIs via MCP as an *additional* channel; standalone paid MCP servers report "hundreds of subscribers" at best (Ref) or zero (several). OpenAI's GPT Store revenue share never generally launched (invite-only pilot, US-only, as of Oct 2024); ChatGPT Apps SDK (Oct 2025) promised monetization via the Agentic Commerce Protocol with terms still undefined; Anthropic's Connectors directory (July 2025) and Claude Marketplace (2026) list for free with no published revenue share.

### Gaps

## Free tiers and abuse controls (objective item)

### Takeaway
Free-tier abuse in generative AI is documented at the scale of thousands of fake signups per fortnight for a single image generator (Castle, 2025); Midjourney killed free trials outright in March 2023 citing "extraordinary abuse". Controls actually observed in the wild: email verification with disposable-domain filtering, SMS verification with virtual-number detection, Cloudflare bot scoring with silent challenges, device fingerprinting + IP-range salt for anonymous daily credits, daily (not monthly) resets, watermarks and resolution caps, non-commercial/CC-BY licensing, download caps, queue priority, and simply charging from day one.

### Cited Findings
- Midjourney ended free trials in late March 2023 after "extraordinary demand and trial abuse" (David Holz), amid viral deepfakes; coverage framed it partly as revenue protection. — [Yahoo/Engadget, 2023-03](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/article/midjourney-ends-free-trials-of-its-ai-image-generator-due-to-extraordinary-abuse-153853905.html)
- Castle (4 Feb 2025): an AI image generator customer with a free plan saw >3,500 malicious registration attempts in two weeks from bots and disposable emails; abusers use disposable email APIs, day-rented temporary phone numbers (verification SMS for Mistral, Poe, Claude, OpenAI observed on such numbers), bulk bot account creation; consequences: GPU cost, distorted growth data, stricter free tiers that hurt real users; recommended: email verification with selective disposable-domain filtering, phone verification with virtual-number checks (beware SMS pumping), selective bot detection, adaptive risk-based friction. Example free limits cited: 10 image generations/day. — [Castle, 2025-02-04](https://blog.castle.io/how-bots-and-fraudsters-exploit-free-tiers-in-ai-saas/)
- zsky.ai (2026): anonymous visitors get N credits/day keyed to a device fingerprint (fingerprintjs + IP-range salt), reset at midnight UTC, stored client- and server-side, not cross-referenced with identity; Cloudflare bot score above threshold → silent challenge/CAPTCHA; anonymous jobs share the queue tagged by fingerprint hash; admitted weakness: rotating fingerprints + residential proxies can get "a few hundred requests" through before heuristics catch them. — [dev.to / zsky.ai, 2026](https://dev.to/zsky/the-anonymous-first-product-bet-why-we-let-40-of-our-users-never-sign-up-3l7g)
- Fingerprint (device intelligence vendor) case studies outside AI: ZBD cut multi-accounting 95%; DreamHost detected duplicate signups/referral abuse; "100+ device and network signals". No generative-AI customer case study found. — [Fingerprint ZBD case study](https://fingerprint.com/case-studies/zbd-reduces-multiaccounting-protects-revenue/)
- Stripe Sessions 2026: a "coordinated multi-account attack" systematically exploited free token usage at an AI company. — [Stripe Sessions 2026](https://stripe.com/cn/sessions/2026/how-ai-changes-the-fight-against-fraud)
- Output-side controls observed: Photoroom free = watermark + no commercial use; Krea free = watermark + no commercial license; UpRes free = corner watermark; Meshy free = CC BY 4.0 attribution license; Tripo free = public models, non-commercial, 15 downloads/month, 10 stored models, 1 concurrent task, standard queue; Topaz = export megapixel caps by tier. — [Photoroom secondary](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/photoroom-pricing-all-plans-and-costs-2026-guide); [Krea secondary](https://zsky.ai/blog/is-krea-free); [Upsampler comparison](https://upsampler.com/blog/best-free-ai-image-upscaler-2026); [Meshy docs](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/pricing); [Costbench Tripo](https://www.costbench.com/software/ai-3d-generation/tripo-ai/); [Topaz pricing](https://www.topazlabs.com/pricing)
- Pieter Levels' stance: charge from day one; free users are "horrible" because of abuse (Photo AI has no free tier). — [PPC Land, 2025-05-25](https://ppc.land/how-one-photo-ai-app-generates-132k-monthly-after-70-failed-startups/)
- Conversion cost of friction (ChartMogul 2026): card-required trials convert 25–35% but yield 35 signups/1,000 visitors vs 90 for freemium; net paying customers per 1,000 visitors: card trial 10.5 vs freemium ~5 vs ungated freemium 5.6. — [Userpilot citing ChartMogul, 2026](https://userpilot.com/blog/free-trial-conversion-rate/)

### Inferences
- For a GPU-heavy 3D tool, the cheapest robust stack is: ungated low-res/watermarked or non-downloadable preview (compute spent, but no asset leaves), daily rather than monthly free credits (caps burst abuse), fingerprint + IP-salted anonymous quota, email verification with disposable-domain blocklist, and card-required trial for full-resolution download. The 3D-specific lever is *download gating* (Rodin pay-on-download, Tripo 15 downloads/month) rather than watermarking, since a watermark on a mesh is impractical.

### Gaps
- No quantified before/after data for any single control (e.g., abuse reduction from adding fingerprinting) at a generative AI company; vendor case studies are from fintech/hosting.
- No disclosure of free-tier compute cost as a share of revenue for a narrow AI tool.

## Lifetime deals (AppSumo) for AI tools (objective item)

### Takeaway
AppSumo LTDs can produce large upfront cash for AI tools (Octolens ~$200K gross in 60 days, 2024; Mootion three campaigns from Oct 2025 with no measured cannibalization), but net payouts are ~20–30% of gross after AppSumo's ~70% Select share and refunds, and the model only works for AI products when lifetime tiers carry *monthly credit caps*. AppSumo itself reported revenue down 50% over two years (Feb 2026), with commentators blaming LTD economics for inference-cost products.

### Cited Findings
- Octolens (AI B2B SaaS, AppSumo Select, Dec 2024): "slightly over $200,000 in total sales within the 60-day period"; net payouts "closer to $40,000" after AppSumo's share (~70% for Octolens; founder's research found 65–80%; Noah Kagan cited an average of 50%) and refunds (60-day money-back); onboarded 1,000+ users; inactivity clause allows disabling accounts after six months idle; "AI costs started to go down quite a bit" enabled LTD tiers; Ahrefs DR rose 10→23; some conversion to "partial MRR" via add-ons. — [Octolens, 2024-12-12](https://octolens.com/blog/200k-in-60-days-honest-review-of-appsumo-select-for-my-ai-b2b-saas-product-2024)
- Mootion (AI animation; AppSumo launch Oct 2025, case study 11 Jun 2026): three campaigns; lifetime tiers include monthly credit caps described as "the cost control that made the model work"; LTD users used 20–30% more credits in months 1–3 then normalized; "no cannibalization" — described as penetrating a price-sensitive segment; LTD buyers more vocal, drove reviews/word of mouth; MRR $100K–$150K and profitable; AppSumo-user active lifetime ~15–18 months matching platform-wide figures; margins improved each campaign as inference costs fell. — [AppSumo blog, 2026-06-11](https://appsumo.com/blog/mootion)
- Pretty Prompt (AI prompt rewriter): 5,200 AppSumo buyers; "every prompt refinement carries a cost" (AppSumo marketing post). — [AppSumo blog](https://appsumo.com/blog/prettyprompt)
- Indie Hackers analysis of 771 AppSumo deals (page now 403; search snippet): ~$4.56M total, average $1,775 per deal to founders (figure as reported in snippet; likely a typo for a larger number — unverified), medians $108K for Select and $41K for Marketplace deals. — [Indie Hackers (snippet)](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/crazy-insights-how-much-revenue-you-can-generate-from-appsumo-9d3efa2e40)
- Revenue splits (founder anecdotes, 2024–2025): Select ≈ 30% to founder / 70% to AppSumo; Marketplace ≈ 70% to founder / 30% to AppSumo; AppSumo says splits are negotiated per partner and partners earn commission on self-referred buyers. — [AppSumo, "Breaking down revenue share"](https://appsumo.com/blog/breaking-down-appsumo-revenue-share); [Indie Hackers thread](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/is-appsumo-select-program-worth-it-2374bdaad7)
- Noah Kagan (X post reported 7 Feb 2026): AppSumo revenue fell 50% over the past two years, attributed to structural shifts in how software is created, distributed and priced; commentators cited LTD adverse selection (vendors fund indefinite support with no recurring revenue), AI making software cheaper to build, and vendor shutdowns leaving buyers without the "got your back" guarantee. — [PPC Land, 2026-02-07](https://ppc.land/appsumos-revenue-crashes-50-as-lifetime-deal-model-faces-existential-crisis/)

### Inferences
- An LTD for a GPU-cost AI tool is viable only as "lifetime access to a plan with monthly credit caps" (Mootion) — i.e., a lifetime subscription to a quota, not unlimited use — and should be modeled at ~25% net of gross.
- The strategic value is distribution (1,000+ users, reviews, backlinks) rather than cash; Octolens' DR 10→23 and Mootion's word-of-mouth are the documented upside.

### Gaps
- No LTD case gives refund rate, support cost per LTD user, or 24-month cohort usage for an AI tool.
- AppSumo's current published split terms were not fetched directly.

## Compute costs and gross-margin disclosures for AI tools (objective item)

### Takeaway
Disclosures are sparse and mostly second-hand: narrow tools with per-use GPU cost report high-80s to 90%+ margins when pricing is $29+/month against ~$0.01–$0.10 per generation (Photo AI ~$12K/month Replicate GPU on ~$130K revenue — unverified Indie Hackers case study); category-level data shows fast-growing AI "Supernovas" at ~25% gross margin (often negative) vs ~60% for steadier AI SaaS (Bessemer 2025), and frontier-model compute margins improving (OpenAI ~35% Jan 2024 → ~70% Oct 2025 per The Information via SaaStr). 3D generation at 20–40 credits ($0.20–$0.40 retail) per model implies thin per-unit margins only if a provider's own inference cost is near that; vendors offset via monthly-reset credits (breakage) and queue-priority tiers.

### Cited Findings
- Photo AI cost structure (Indie Hackers case study, undated, unverified): total ~$13K/month — Replicate GPU ~$12K/month, VPS ~$40/month, other ~$1K/month; "profit margin 87%+" against $132K/month revenue (May 2025). PPC Land's own article says margins were not disclosed. — [Indie Hackers case study (snippet)](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/photo-ai-by-pieter-levels-complete-deep-dive-case-study-0-to-132k-mrr-in-18-months-3a9a2b1579); [PPC Land, 2025-05-25](https://ppc.land/how-one-photo-ai-app-generates-132k-monthly-after-70-failed-startups/)
- Levels has said his margins are high "except Photo AI and Interior AI because they have GPU costs"; an InteriorAI write-up claims >99% margin because no per-user model training is needed (secondary, unverified). — [proearnly InteriorAI, 2025/2026](https://proearnly.pages.dev/articles/interiorai-170k-month-solo-ai-tool)
- Bessemer State of AI 2025 (20 companies): "Supernovas" ~25% gross margins (often negative) with near-vertical growth; "Shooting Stars" ~60% gross margins, ~$3M ARR year 1 → ~$103M ARR year 4. — [Bessemer State of AI 2025 (mirror)](https://openlinksw.com/data/html/bessemer-ventures-ai-2025-report-claude.html); [Confluence VC summary](https://confluencevcweekly.beehiiv.com/p/negative-gross-margins)
- OpenAI "compute margin" ~35% (Jan 2024) → ~70% (Oct 2025) per The Information, as relayed by SaaStr; Anthropic gross-margin forecasts conflict (50% expected / cut to 40% for 2025 / 77% by 2028). — [SaaStr, 2025](https://www.saastr.com/have-ai-gross-margins-really-turned-the-corner/)
- Bain Capital Ventures: compute costs for AI apps run roughly 1–3x traditional software hosting costs. — [BCV, "Gross margin is a BS metric"](https://baincapitalventures.com/insight/gross-margin-is-a-bs-metric/)
- Canaan: "SaaS trained us to ignore marginal cost; AI won't." (essay, 2025). — [Canaan](https://canaan.com/latest/saas-trained-us-to-ignore-marginal-cost-ai-won-t)
- Stage 2 / Poyar: top 10% of users may drive 70–80% of AI costs; MoviePass flat-rate "death spiral" as cautionary tale. — [Stage 2 Capital, 2025-11-15](https://gtm.stage2.capital/p/credits-arent-a-strategy-rethinking)
- Mootion: margins improved across three AppSumo campaigns "as inference costs fell" (Oct 2025–mid 2026). — [AppSumo blog, 2026-06-11](https://appsumo.com/blog/mootion)
- Small paid MCP server: $14/month data costs, cited 80% gross margin at $19/month (self-reported, 2026). — [dev.to, 2026](https://dev.to/whoffagents/pricing-an-mcp-server-in-2026-why-we-charge-19mo-when-the-market-average-is-0-nig)
- Retail price of 3D generation as a ceiling on provider cost: Meshy ~$0.40/model and Tripo ~$0.21/model on entry tiers (Sept 2025); Rodin $1.50/credit pay-on-download; Kaedim ~$20/geometry with human review. — [Sloyd, 2025-09-04](https://sloyd.ai/blog/3d-ai-price-comparison); [Costbench Rodin/Kaedim](https://www.costbench.com/compare/kaedim-vs-rodin-hyper3d/)
- Cost-side controls visible on pricing pages: monthly credits that expire (Meshy, Topaz, Magnific-era Freepik), queue priority and concurrency limits by tier (Meshy, Tripo), inactivity clauses on LTDs (Octolens), fair-use caps on "unlimited" tools (Photoroom). — see sources above

### Inferences
- A narrow tool that wraps third-party inference (Replicate/fal/Meshy API) can expect 80–90% gross margin only if ARPU is ≥$20/month and median usage stays well below the credit allowance; the risk is concentrated in the top-10% power users (70–80% of cost), which is exactly what monthly-reset credits, concurrency limits and priority queues are designed to contain.
- For 3D specifically, reselling at $0.20–$0.40/model leaves little room; a wrapper should price per *accepted* asset at $1.50+ (Rodin's pay-on-download) or bundle human QA at $20+ (Kaedim) rather than compete on raw per-generation price.

### Gaps
- No single-purpose AI tool (image, 3D, audio) has published audited gross margin; all figures are founder statements or third-party estimates.
- Meshy/Tripo/Rodin do not disclose their own inference cost per model; Shopify 3D/AR app developers disclose nothing.
